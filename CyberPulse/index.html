02 / Activity & response

Threat intelligence

A reported claim, an investigation, and guidance rooted in a historical case. The distinctions matter.

A01
InvestigationSeptember 23, 2026

What happened?

On September 23, 2026, the FBI said it was investigating a cybercriminal group’s claim that the fbijobs.gov portal had been compromised. The group also alleged that FBI employee personally identifiable information (PII) was affected. These are the group’s claims as reported by the FBI, not facts the FBI confirmed in that statement.

What is known—and unknown?

The FBI said the point of breach had not been determined. It was examining whether the issue involved a third-party provider or the FBI’s own enterprise and was working with supporting third-party providers to mitigate risk. The statement identifies no group, entry point, affected-person count, or data scope. It does not confirm that the portal was compromised or that PII was accessed or taken.

We checked the FBI cyber-news listing through September 29 and found no public follow-up there. That check cannot establish that no update exists elsewhere.

Why it matters

The unresolved question about a provider versus an internal system is relevant to incident investigation and supplier oversight. At this stage, readers should keep the alleged impact distinct from confirmed facts; the cited statement does not give affected individuals or administrators a technical remediation procedure.

Editorial status: The compromise claim and alleged PII impact remain unconfirmed in the cited FBI statement. Do not infer that employee records were exposed.

A02
ICS / OTGuidance · September 23, 2026

What the guidance covers

Industrial control systems (ICS) are the hardware and software used to monitor or control industrial processes. Operational technology (OT) is the broader environment that interacts with physical equipment. Organizations often rely on third-party integrators for system design, installation, data analysis, device support, or operational services. That work can require access to sensitive networks, systems, and data.

In a fact sheet dated September 23, 2026, FBI and CISA ask critical-infrastructure operators to assess integrator access and data handling, limit privileges to what a task needs, monitor remote connections, and include cybersecurity and supply-chain requirements in contracts. They also recommend maintaining software and hardware inventories, secure offline backups, and rehearsed manual-operation and recovery procedures.

Historical context, not a new incident

The fact sheet attributes a separate March–April 2025 incident to FBI technical analysis. It says foreign malicious actors accessed the network of an unnamed U.S. industrial automation company serving industrial customers, including power utilities and transportation entities. The actors created nine ZIP files containing about 800 files for presumed exfiltration. The document does not say the files were successfully transferred or confirm resulting theft.

How the recommendations reduce risk

Least privilege limits what an integrator account can reach if it is misused. Monitoring remote access helps operators notice unexpected sessions. Contract terms make security duties and reporting expectations explicit. Inventories help teams understand which equipment and software need protection, while offline backups and practiced recovery give operators options if systems are disrupted. The agencies say these measures should be tailored to the organization and operational-safety needs.

Time distinction: The guidance was published in September 2026. The incident it discusses occurred in March–April 2025. “Presumed exfiltration” does not establish confirmed data theft.

03 / Exposure & remediation

Vulnerability watch

Five CISA KEV additions in the approved Section 4 draft. Deadlines and descriptions follow the cited catalog and vendor sources.

CVE-2026-86950

Apple · iOS, iPadOS & macOS

Upcoming · Oct 2

Apple describes an out-of-bounds write in CoreGraphics. In plain terms, a program writes beyond the memory area intended for that operation. Apple says a crafted file may trigger the flaw and allow arbitrary code execution.

Scope and evidence

Apple says it is aware of a report that this issue may have been exploited in a sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals. This is Apple’s qualified statement; it does not identify victims or establish broader exploitation. Apple’s security advisories dated September 28 list fixes in iOS and iPadOS 26.7.1, macOS Tahoe 26.7.1, and macOS Sequoia 15.8.1. CISA added the CVE the next day, September 29.

What to do

Apple’s advisories provide the affected product updates and remediation. CISA lists an October 2 due date. As of September 29, that deadline was upcoming. Apply the vendor update appropriate to each Apple device.

Apple advisories: September 28, 2026 · CISA KEV addition: September 29, 2026 · Due October 2, 2026.

CVE-2026-88771

Citrix · NetScaler ADC / Gateway

Upcoming · Sep 30

Citrix describes improper input validation: input is not sufficiently checked before the product processes it. Under the advisory’s description, an unauthenticated remote attacker could execute arbitrary commands. Citrix reports observed exploitation on unmitigated deployments.

Conditions and scope

Citrix says no special feature needs to be enabled for CVE-2026-88771 to affect supported NetScaler ADC and Gateway deployments. Its bulletin does not provide a detailed exploit sequence; this explanation does not imply one. Citrix says its managed cloud services were upgraded by Citrix.

Remediation and timing

Citrix’s bulletin was first published September 27 and lists fixed releases including 14.1-73.37 or later and 13.1-64.23 or later, with separate FIPS and NDcPP builds. The checked bulletin does not give a separate release date for each fixed build. CISA added the CVE on September 27 and lists a September 30 due date, which was upcoming as of September 29. Use the exact build applicable to the deployment.

CVE-2026-88772

Citrix · NetScaler ADC / Gateway

Upcoming · Sep 30

A memory overflow occurs when a program handles more data than the relevant memory area can safely contain. Citrix says this flaw may allow remote code execution or denial of service when DTLS is enabled. DTLS is enabled by default on a VPN virtual server, according to the bulletin.

Conditions and scope

Citrix identifies supported NetScaler ADC and Gateway deployments with the DTLS condition. It reports observed exploitation on unmitigated deployments. Citrix says its managed cloud services were upgraded by Citrix.

Remediation and timing

Citrix’s bulletin was first published September 27 and lists fixed builds for the affected release branches, including 14.1-73.37 or later and 13.1-64.23 or later, with separate FIPS and NDcPP builds. The checked bulletin does not give a separate release date for each fixed build. CISA added this CVE on September 27 and lists a September 30 due date, upcoming as of September 29. Verify the required build and DTLS exposure against the vendor advisory.

CVE-2026-87902

WordPress · Core

Past due · Sep 28

The WordPress developer advisory describes an unauthenticated path-traversal flaw involving how a page template is selected. Under specific theme and server conditions, an attacker may cause WordPress to include a chosen local PHP file that the web server can read, potentially enabling remote code execution.

Conditions and limits

The advisory says the active parent or child theme must include a top-level directory whose name begins with page-, and the selected local PHP file must exist and be readable by the web server. Additional server configuration affects whether the chain can result in code execution. The advisory does not say every WordPress site is exploitable.

Remediation and timing

The developer advisory was published September 22 and lists fixed releases for supported branches from 4.7.37 through 7.1.2; administrators should use the patched version for their branch. CISA added the CVE on September 25 and set September 28 as the due date. That date had passed by September 29; it does not tell us whether a particular site has been updated.

CVE-2026-71362

Adobe · Commerce and Magento Open Source

Past due · Sep 27

Adobe classifies this as incorrect authorization: the product does not correctly enforce whether a request is allowed. Adobe lists privilege escalation as the impact and says authentication and administrator privileges are not required to exploit it.

Products and updates

Adobe’s APSB26-92 covers Adobe Commerce and Magento Open Source. The bulletin lists affected Commerce versions 2.4.4 through 2.4.9, including the July 2026 releases and earlier; Magento Open Source coverage begins at 2.4.6. Adobe’s August 2026 updated releases include Commerce 2.4.4-2026-aug through 2.4.9-2026-aug and Magento Open Source 2.4.6-2026-aug through 2.4.9-2026-aug. Check the bulletin for the exact product and installation instructions.

Dates and exploitation statements

Adobe’s bulletin was first published August 11 and last updated August 18, 2026. Adobe said it was not aware of in-the-wild exploits at that time. CISA added the CVE to KEV on September 24 and lists September 27 as the remediation due date; that date had passed by September 29. These are statements from different sources on different dates, not a single contemporaneous assessment.

Reading the record: Adobe’s August 18 statement describes Adobe’s awareness at that time. CISA’s September 24 addition is a later catalog fact. Neither date establishes whether a particular organization has remediated.

CVE-2026-95985AWS advisory · Sep 24, 2026

Kiro IDE’s untrusted-workspace boundary

What happened?

AWS disclosed CVE-2026-95985 in Kiro, a desktop development environment with an AI agent. AWS says versions before 1.0.242 may allow a remote unauthenticated actor to execute commands or inject crafted instructions into the agent’s context under a specific set of conditions.

How the scenario works

The user first opens a crafted repository as an untrusted workspace and runs the Kiro agent. The user then sends a message. In that situation, agent actions could affect auto-loaded global configuration paths. “Untrusted workspace” means the project should not be treated as safe merely because it is open in the IDE. This is not a claim that opening any repository automatically triggers the issue: the bulletin describes the agent-and-message interaction as part of the scenario.

Global configuration can affect Kiro beyond one project. AWS therefore recommends reviewing those settings if the affected scenario occurred on an earlier version.

Who is affected, and what to do?

The bulletin concerns Kiro IDE versions before 1.0.242. It does not establish that all AWS cloud services or deployments are affected. AWS recommends upgrading to the latest Kiro version. If an agent was run in an untrusted workspace with an earlier version, inspect the global Kiro configuration directory for entries you did not create. AWS identifies ~/.kiro on macOS and Linux and %USERPROFILE%\.kiro on Windows.

Evidence limit: The checked AWS bulletin describes a possible attack path but does not state whether exploitation in the wild occurred.

05 / Database extensions

Cloud security

AWS’s advisory is explicit about what is—and is not—in scope.

CVE-2026-96883 · PostgreSQL extensionAffected self-deployed builds

pgcollection type confusion: a narrow deployment scope

What happened?

CVE-2026-96883 affects pgcollection, an open-source extension for PostgreSQL. AWS and the project’s GitHub Security Advisory identify versions 2.0.0 through 2.1.1 as affected. The issue is type confusion: when a stored icollection value is requested as an incompatible type, the extension can interpret the underlying data incorrectly.

Prerequisites and potential impact

The advisory says an authenticated database user can trigger the condition. The stated impact is a PostgreSQL backend crash or arbitrary code execution. The sources do not say the issue is unauthenticated and do not provide evidence about in-the-wild exploitation; that status is not stated in the checked materials.

Who is affected?

AWS identifies customers who downloaded pgcollection 2.0.0–2.1.1 from the AWS repository, compiled it, and deployed it in their own PostgreSQL environments. This is a specific self-managed deployment path, not a finding about every PostgreSQL installation or every cloud deployment.

✓

AWS excludes existing Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL and Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL customers. The advisories say these services ship pgcollection 1.1.1, which does not contain this issue. This finding should not be generalized to all PostgreSQL or cloud deployments.

07 / Plain-language reference

Issue 001 glossary

Short definitions for technical terms used in this issue. A term’s presence here does not add evidence about any specific incident.

CVE
A public identifier used to refer to a specific cybersecurity vulnerability. The identifier itself does not describe severity or prove that a particular system is affected.
CISA KEV
The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency’s catalog of known exploited vulnerabilities. A catalog entry is a CISA record; technical details should still be checked against the original vendor advisory.
Remote code execution (RCE)
A vulnerability outcome in which an attacker can cause a system to run code or commands remotely. The access, authentication, and configuration requirements depend on the specific flaw.
Type confusion
A software error where a program handles data as though it had a different type or structure than it actually has. The consequences depend on how the affected code uses that data.
Untrusted workspace
A project folder or repository that an IDE or user treats as potentially unsafe. That label is a trust boundary; it does not by itself mean the project is malicious.
ICS / OT
Industrial control systems monitor or control industrial processes. Operational technology describes the hardware and software that interacts with physical equipment and operations.
Least privilege
Give a person, service, or device only the access needed for its assigned task, and limit that access where practical.
Presumed exfiltration
Files were prepared or staged with an apparent intent to transfer them out. The phrase does not establish that the transfer succeeded or that data theft was confirmed.
